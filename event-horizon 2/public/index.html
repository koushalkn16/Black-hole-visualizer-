01 · The idea in 30 seconds

What is happening to light?

Mass curves spacetime. Light always travels the straightest path it can — but near a black hole the straightest path is curved.

  1. Far away, light bends. The closer it passes, the harder it turns.
  2. At 1.5 Rs light can orbit. This is the photon sphere — a knife-edge where light circles the hole.
  3. Any closer, and it is gone. Aim inside the critical distance bc = 2.598 Rs and the beam spirals across the event horizon.

That one number, bc, sets the size of the black hole's shadow — the dark disc the Event Horizon Telescope observed in 2017 and published in 2022.

Scientific explanation

For a non-rotating (Schwarzschild) black hole of mass M, a light ray with impact parameter b obeys the exact orbit equation d²u/dφ² + u = 3GMu²/c², with u = 1/r. Without the right-hand side, the solution is a straight line; that term is the whole of general relativity's effect on the path.

Light behaves as if it moves in an effective potential V(r) = (1 − Rs/r)/r², which peaks at r = 1.5 Rs (the photon sphere). A beam whose "energy level" 1/b² is above that peak goes over the barrier and falls in. Setting 1/bc² = Vmax gives the exact critical value bc = (3√3/2) Rs.

04 · The Gravity Lab

Newton vs Einstein

Fix the beam. Change the black hole. See where Newton's gravity and Einstein's disagree — and by how much.

General relativityNewton (light as a fast particle)
—
Assumptions of each model

Newton. Light is treated as a particle moving at speed c in flat space with absolute time, pulled by an inverse-square force. Its path is an exact hyperbola: deflection 2·arcsin(1/e), with e = √(1 + (bc²/GM)²). Newton has no horizon and no photon sphere — but Michell (1783) noticed that a "dark star" traps light inside 2GM/c², the same Rs.

Einstein. Light follows a null geodesic of the Schwarzschild spacetime (non-spinning, uncharged black hole, observer far away). Path from RK4, checked against Darwin's exact formula.

Why they differ. In general relativity space itself is curved as well as time, and the curvature grows faster than 1/r² close in. Far away this simply doubles the bending; near the hole it creates the photon sphere and the horizon, which have no Newtonian counterpart.
